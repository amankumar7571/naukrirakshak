Slide 02 · Problem

Freshers are losing money to jobs that don’t exist

Every day, Indian freshers lose ₹5,000–₹50,000 to fake job offers — “refundable security deposits”, WhatsApp HR messages, and offer letters issued with no interview at all.

“Congratulations! You are selected. Pay ₹2,500 refundable security deposit to confirm your seat and get the offer letter today.”

A textbook scam pattern — fee first, urgency second, no real company anywhere.
₹5k–50k

Lost per victim to deposits, “registration fees” and processing charges that are never refunded.

It arrives on WhatsApp

No official email, no careers page — just a personal chat, a UPI ID, and pressure to pay before you can think.

Nothing checks it

Portals list jobs; nobody verifies the message already sitting in a fresher’s phone. That gap is the product.

Problem: fraud reaches the fresher before any platform does.02 / 10
Slide 03 · Who it hurts

The people least able to verify are the ones targeted

Scammers don’t target experienced engineers with networks. They target first-generation job seekers who have no one to ask, “does this look real?”

10M+

Graduates enter the Indian job market every year — most hunting on phones, many on WhatsApp.

First-generation seekers

No family member has ever received a real offer letter — so a fake one looks exactly like success.

Small-town students

Far from campus placement cells and alumni networks, a polished PDF and a confident “HR” voice is all they get.

  • This is personal: the builder has received these scam messages himself — consultancy spam and “HR” offers asking for money and documents upfront.
  • The cost isn’t only money: Aadhaar copies, bank details and OTPs shared with fraudsters follow a fresher for years.
Audience: every fresher with a phone and a job alert.03 / 10
Slide 04 · Solution

Paste the message. Get a verdict — with receipts

NaukriRakshak checks any job message, posting or offer letter in seconds and shows exactly why it is risky, in plain language a first-generation job seeker can act on.

1

Paste anything

A WhatsApp HR message, an email, a portal listing, an offer letter.

2

Risk score 0–100%

A trained classifier scores it instantly — green, amber or red.

3

Evidence, highlighted

The exact suspicious phrases are marked in your text, each with its reason.

4

A clear next step

“Likely scam”, “No scam signs found”, or — when unsure — 3 plain-language checks to run first.

  • Works fully offline in the browser — no sign-in, no fee, no API key, no server. The demo cannot die on stage.
  • Shows uncertainty honestly: amber means “we’re not sure — verify these 3 things”, never a fake-confident guess.
  • Green is worded carefully: “No scam signs found” only means the message contains no known scam words — verify on the company’s official careers page before sharing any document.
Solution: a 10-second check before a fresher pays, shares, or trusts.04 / 10
Slide 05 · How the AI works

Real machine learning — not a chatbot wrapper

Two stages. The score comes from a classifier trained for this exact task; the explanation shows its evidence. No prompt is doing the judging.

Stage 1 · Trained model

TF-IDF + Logistic Regression

scikit-learn model trained on 117 labelled scam / genuine examples (word 1–2 grams). The exact exported weights — intercept + the full 2,063-term vocabulary, every coefficient and idf value — run in the browser with exact TF-IDF scoring.

→
Stage 2 · Evidence

Every flag, explained

Deterministic phrase-flagging highlights fee demands, urgency, WhatsApp-only HR, free-mail domains — each with a plain-language reason.

→
Verdict

Score + reasons + action

Risk band, highlighted text, money-at-risk amount, and a verification checklist when the model is uncertain.

  • Production explainer: Gemini turns the flags into a fuller plain-language explanation in the deployed version.
  • Demo explainer: an offline version ships with the prototype, so the finale demo never depends on an API quota.
  • Stack: Python · scikit-learn · TF-IDF + logistic regression · Gemini (explanations) · runs client-side.
Judges penalise ChatGPT wrappers. This is a trained classifier with exported, inspectable weights.05 / 10

    Judge questions — rehearsed answers

    The four attacks this project will get, and the answers already built into the product.

    “Isn’t this just a ChatGPT wrapper?”

    No — the verdict comes from a logistic-regression classifier trained on labelled scam/genuine examples. Its exported weights run in the browser; you can inspect every coefficient. Gemini only explains, it never judges.

    “24/24 — isn’t that test set tiny?”

    Yes, and the deck says so on the results slide. It’s a representative held-out set, real-world accuracy will be lower — that’s why uncertain cases get an amber verification checklist instead of a confident verdict.

    “Scammers will just change their wording — data drift?”

    That’s what the Report button is for: reported messages arrive with user labels, feeding human-in-the-loop retraining. The roadmap’s first item is exactly this data flywheel.

    “What if the demo network dies?”

    It can’t — scoring and explanations run fully offline in the browser. No API key, no server, no quota in the demo path.
